# Marketing plan — Louisiana Industrial Readiness

## Positioning

**For** Louisiana operating companies that already do industrial work  
**who** keep losing time (or invites) because the packet is incomplete  
**LIR** is a document-packaging practice  
**that** turns existing proof into a file a prime can move on  
**unlike** job boards, bid-matching apps, and “I know a guy at the plant” brokers.

Promise: *If a prime pulled your file tonight, they would find a company they can buy from.*

Anti-promise: we do not sell access, introductions, or awards.

## Brand rules

- Industrial letterhead. Paper, near-black ink, Gulf blue. No SaaS gradients.
- Speak like a cover sheet, not a startup.
- Always disclose: not affiliated with SpaceX / Tesla / xAI / NASA / LED / the State.
- Always disclose: not legal / insurance / engineering / estimating advice.
- Show a sample memo and one honest proof page. Do not fake logos of primes.

## Offers and funnel

```
cold note / group comment / search
        ↓
  /checklist  (email captured)
        ↓
  $249 Audit  (48 hours)
        ↓
  $995 Proof Pack  ($249 credit for 14 days)
        ↓
  Bid Desk when an invite lands
        ↓
  public /p/<slug> that sends you quote requests
```

The audit is the wedge. The Proof Pack is the product. Bid Desk is surge revenue. The public proof page is the quiet retainer-shaped asset.

## Pricing rationale (do not discount on the first call)

- $249 is cheap enough that an owner will try it without a meeting
- $995 is a real invoice that funds two revision rounds
- Bid Desk tiers exist so a panicked Friday invite does not become unpaid consulting

If someone wants “just a logo and a flyer,” decline. That is a different business.

## Channels that work for this (in order)

1. **Direct relationships** — 20 contractors you already know
2. **One honest comment** in parish / trade / “who’s hiring for the plant” threads
3. **Checklist SEO** — “Louisiana contractor prequalification checklist”, “COI for industrial bid Louisiana”
4. **Proof pages** — each published `/p/<slug>` is a landing page that can send work back to the contractor *and* leads back to you
5. **Accountant / insurance-agent referrals** — they see the stale COI first. Offer them a $50 thank-you on a closed Proof Pack, disclosed
6. Paid ads later, if ever. Google search on “contractor prequalification packet Louisiana” is the only paid test worth running in month two

Channels that waste time: TikTok brand dances, generic “we help businesses grow,” claiming the SpaceX news cycle.

## 90-day plan

### Days 1–14 — Open the shop
- Domain + Stripe test + Resend
- 20 personalized emails
- 1 group comment per week, max
- Publish `/p/demo` only if the sample company facts stay labeled as a sample
- Goal: 10 checklist leads, 1 paid audit

### Days 15–45 — Proof
- Deliver audits in under 48 hours every time
- Convert audits to Proof Packs with the credit clock
- Ask every delivered audit client for one vendor-side intro (“who else is bidding the same work?”)
- Goal: 8 audits, 2 Proof Packs

### Days 46–90 — Repeatable
- One case study written from a real delivered pack (client-approved, no invented numbers)
- Start a monthly “packet stale?” note to past buyers
- Add Bid Desk only after you have the audit motion working
- Goal: $8k–$15k gross, or an honest read that the offer needs a different parish / trade focus

## Copy blocks you can reuse

“Ten categories. Red, yellow, green. A fix list you can act on in 48 hours.”

Proof Pack one-liner:  
“A capabilities sheet, project sheets, and a public page built from facts you already have.”

Bid Desk one-liner:  
“Forward the invite. We map what they asked for and assemble the folder. You submit.”

## Measurement

Track only:

- Checklist emails
- Paid audits
- Audit → Proof Pack conversion inside 14 days
- Hours per audit (target under 2.5)
- Hours per Proof Pack (target under 8)
- Refunds and refusals (write down why)

If conversion is under 20% after 10 audits, the memo is not making the next step obvious. Fix the “recommended next step” line before you buy ads.
